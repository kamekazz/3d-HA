# Round 1 blind verdicts — 9 critics, 9 FAILs

Every critic was shown two unlabelled images in shuffled order and asked which was
the photograph. **All nine picked the photograph, all nine said `certain`.** Nobody
found it hard. Below, each critic's own words, with the labels decoded: wherever a
critic says our render, it is written as OURS.


## kneewall_a — verdict FAIL (critic named the photograph, certain)
**Subject:** the low white knee wall guarding the stairwell, and its top cap

**What gave it away in two seconds:** On the subject itself: OURS' knee-wall cap is a chunky uniform slab with hard, noise-free highlights and zero contact shadow where the wall meets the floor, while A's cap is a thin trim board with a real shadow line under its nosing and visible sensor grain across its white face.

**THE SINGLE BIGGEST GAP:** The cap is modelled as a slab instead of a trim board. In OURS the top cap is roughly 3-4 in thick and overhangs both faces with a fat square lip, reading as a poured bench top; its underside is a bright hard line with no shadow, so the cap looks glued on. In A (mid-left, running from the door jamb down to the near end) the cap is a thin flat board — about 1 in of visible edge with a small eased nosing — and the underside of that nosing throws a continuous dark shadow line the full length of the wall, which is what separates cap from wall face. Cut cap thickness to about a third, shrink the overhang, and add a real occlusion line under it.

**Then, ranked:**

- The near end is wrong geometry. A terminates in one clean vertical stub end with the cap returning square over it, stopping around 60% down the frame. OURS mushrooms into a stepped blocky assembly at bottom-left — an extra lower return wall running off to the left at a different height with a dark rounded lump on top — and runs out of the bottom of the frame. None of that exists in the photo.
- No base trim on the knee wall in OURS. In A the hallway face carries a white baseboard the full length that mitres around the end onto the floor, with a shadow gap under it; OURS' face runs straight into the floor as a single flat plane, so it reads as a cardboard fin rather than framed-and-trimmed carpentry.
- No contact shadow or tonal falloff. In A the wall face is brighter near the ceiling cans and darkens toward the floor, with a soft dark band on the floor at its base. OURS' face is one uniform pale value top to bottom and the floor is equally lit right up to the wall line, so the wall does not sit in the room.
- The stairwell the wall guards is exposed in B: bottom-left, past the wall, you see lit stair treads and an open drop. In A the wall plus the floor edge fully occludes the stairwell — nothing below the cap line but wall and floor. Related: OURS' cap top edge kinks where the near block meets the long run, while A's is one straight converging line.

## doors_a — verdict FAIL (critic named the photograph, certain)
**Subject:** the white doors: leaf panelling, casing trim, handles, hinges, and the shadow line where the leaf meets the frame

**What gave it away in two seconds:** OURS' surfaces are dead flat — the door leaves are untextured fills with no sensor grain, no paint sheen, no soft falloff. Every edge is a hard aliased vector line, and three chunky black rectangles sit pasted on each leaf like decals. A has grain, a satin sheen gradient across each leaf, and blown highlights on the trim.

**THE SINGLE BIGGEST GAP:** Hinges. OURS puts three fat black rectangles on the FACE of each leaf, near the top, middle and bottom of the hinge stile — the right-hand door's top one at roughly 22% frame height and its bottom one at ~82% are the loudest black marks in the picture, and the left door carries a matching set. Real butt hinges are almost invisible from this angle: in A the hinges on the left door read as two thin dark slivers ~4px wide tucked INSIDE the jamb gap at the leaf's right edge, knuckle-only, mostly occluded by the stop and the casing return. Fix: move the hinge geometry off the leaf face into the reveal between leaf and jamb, cut its visible width to a knuckle sliver, and stop drawing it as an opaque black quad — it should be a barrel catching a specular streak, not a sticker.

**Then, ranked:**

- Panel bevel geometry is grossly oversized. OURS' raised panels use a wide flat chamfer eating ~25-30% of each panel's width, with a single hard crease and no sticking, so the panel field shrinks to a small plateau — reads as CNC'd MDF. A's six-panel doors have a narrow ogee sticking, roughly 10% of panel width, with a rounded shoulder that produces a bright highlight line and a thin cove shadow immediately beside it. Narrow the bevel and give it a curved section.
- The shadow line where leaf meets frame is missing on the right-hand door. In OURS the leaf's left edge (x~430) goes white-leaf straight into white-jamb with only a pale grey step — no gap, no occlusion, and no dark line under the door at the threshold either. In A the equivalent junctions carry a crisp near-black 2-3px reveal running the full leaf height, plus a graded occlusion smudge into the jamb rebate and a dark shadow under the leaf bottom.
- Lever handles are crude and mis-scaled. OURS' are flat L-shaped black extrusions with no rose/escutcheon, no thickness at the mount, and no latch face on the leaf edge; the right door's lever sits low and its shank projects at the wrong angle. A's levers have a visible round rose, a lever that tapers and thickens toward the shank, a specular kick along its top, and the left door additionally carries a separate deadbolt/turn cylinder above the lever — OURS has nothing there.
- Casing trim is inconsistent and half-dissolved. On OURS' right door the head casing is a single thin dark line and the side casing barely separates from the wall; on OURS' left door the casing is a wide flat grey band with no mitre at the corner. A shows uniform square-edge casing ~2.5in wide on every opening, a clear 45-degree mitre at each head corner, a bright top edge and a dark shadow line where the outer edge stands proud of the wall.
